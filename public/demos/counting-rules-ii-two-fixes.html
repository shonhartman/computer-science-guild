Two Fixes: Subtract and Divide

Rule of the hour: Shaun says a number out loud — and how he got it — before any answer is revealed. Wrong guesses are the good part. This whole session is about counts that look right and are not.

Fix one — things overlap, so subtract

~13 min

When groups overlap, adding their sizes counts the shared part more than once. Add the groups, take away every two-way overlap, add back every three-way overlap.

Warm-up: pizza and tacos

Shaun guesses

How many people are there in total?

7, not 9. The 2 both-lovers got counted twice in 5 + 4. Take them out once: 5 + 4 − 2 = 7. Regions: 3 pizza-only + 2 both + 2 tacos-only.

Three circles: where does the middle go?

Shaun guesses

Add all three circles, then subtract all three pairwise overlaps. How many times is the middle region now counted?

Zero. It was added 3 times (once per circle) and subtracted 3 times (once per pair). It vanished — so add it back once. That is the whole pattern: add singles, subtract pairs, add back triples.

The real problem: digits 0–9 in a row

Line up the digits 0 through 9, each used once. How many lineups have 42, 04, or 60 sitting side by side (in that order)?

Step 1 — glue "60" into one tile. Now the row is 9 tiles:

6012345789
Shaun guesses

How many lineups of 9 tiles?

9! = 362,880 — nine choices for the first spot, eight for the next, and so on. Same count for gluing "42", same for "04".

Step 2 — two at once. Gluing 42 and 60 together leaves 8 tiles, so 8! lineups.

Shaun guesses

What about 60 and 04 together?

They share the 0 — so they fuse into one tile, 604. Still 8 tiles, still 8! = 40,320. (Same for 42 and 04: they share the 4 and fuse into "042". All three pairs give 8!.)

Step 3 — all three at once: one tile 6042, so 7 tiles → 7! = 5,040.

Step 4 — assemble with the fix:

3 · 9!  −  3 · 8!  +  7!  =  1,088,640 − 120,960 + 5,040  =  972,720

Trap to name

Stopping after the subtraction. With three groups, the middle got taken out too many times — if you forget the “+ 7!”, you are undercounting.

Fix one — extra rounds

bonus game

More reps for the guessing game. Two rounds with two groups, two with three, and one grand finale with four.

Round 1 (two groups): the sports class

A class of 30 kids. 18 play football, 15 play basketball, and every kid plays at least one of the two.

Shaun guesses

How many kids play both?

3. Same formula, run backwards. 18 + 15 = 33 counts the both-players twice, but there are only 30 kids — so the double-counted part is 33 − 30 = 3. The overlap is whatever the naive sum overshoots by.

Round 2 (two groups): numbers 1–20

How many numbers from 1 to 20 are divisible by 2 or by 3?

Shaun guesses

Divisible by 2: 10 numbers. Divisible by 3: 6 numbers. So… 16?

13, not 16. The numbers divisible by both — that's divisible by 6: the numbers 6, 12, 18 — got counted twice. 10 + 6 − 3 = 13. (Check by hand: 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20.)

Round 3 (three groups): the ice cream survey

25 kids asked which flavors they like. Chocolate: 12. Vanilla: 10. Strawberry: 8. Chocolate + vanilla: 5. Chocolate + strawberry: 4. Vanilla + strawberry: 3. All three: 2.

Shaun guesses

How many kids like at least one flavor — and how many like none?

Add singles, subtract pairs, add back the triple: (12 + 10 + 8) − (5 + 4 + 3) + 2 = 30 − 12 + 2 = 20 like at least one, so 5 like none. The all-three kids were added 3 times, removed 3 times, then rescued by the +2.

Round 4 (three groups): numbers 1–30

How many numbers from 1 to 30 are divisible by 2, 3, or 5?

Shaun guesses

By 2: 15. By 3: 10. By 5: 6. Build the rest of the formula out loud.

Pairs are the shared multiples: by 6: 5, by 10: 3, by 15: 2. Triple is by 30: 1.

(15 + 10 + 6) − (5 + 3 + 2) + 1 = 31 − 10 + 1 = 22

Cross-check: the survivors are 1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29 — exactly 8 of them, and 30 − 8 = 22. Counted two ways, same number.

Finale (four groups): numbers 1–100

How many numbers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 2, 3, 5, or 7?

Shaun guesses

First: with four groups, what does the pattern of plus and minus look like?

The signs keep alternating: add singles, subtract pairs, add triples, subtract quadruples.

singles: 50 + 33 + 20 + 14 = 117

pairs (6, 10, 14, 15, 21, 35): 16 + 10 + 7 + 6 + 4 + 2 = 45

triples (30, 42, 70, 105): 3 + 2 + 1 + 0 = 6

quadruple (210): 0  (210 > 100 — a term can be zero, the formula doesn't mind)

117 − 45 + 6 − 0 = 78

Why alternating works — follow one number that sits in all four groups. Singles add it 4 times. Pairs subtract it 6 times (that's how many pairs you can form from 4 groups). Triples add it 4 times. The quadruple subtracts it once. 4 − 6 + 4 − 1 = 1. Counted exactly once, like everything else. Each +/− layer repairs the layer before it, one step deeper into the overlaps.

Fix two — orders repeat, so divide

~12 min

To count arrangements when some pieces are identical, count as if every piece were different, then divide by the ways to shuffle each set of twins.

Start tiny: A, B, B

If the two B's were different colors, there would be 6 lineups:

A B B A B B B A B B A B B B A B B A
Shaun guesses

Strip the colors. How many lineups can you actually tell apart?

3: ABB, BAB, BBA. Each real lineup appears twice in the colored list — once per way to shuffle the two B's. 6 ÷ 2 = 3. That division is the whole fix.

The doughnut payoff

Left hanging last session: order 12 doughnuts from 5 kinds. Any order is a row of 12 doughnuts and 4 dividers between kinds — 16 symbols:

●●●|●●●●|●|●●|●●

= 3 glazed, 4 chocolate, 1 jelly, 2 sprinkle, 2 plain. Every order is exactly one such row; every row is exactly one order.

Shaun guesses

So: how many ways to place the 4 dividers among 16 positions?

Place them one by one: 16 · 15 · 14 · 13 = 43,680. But the dividers are identical twins — every set of 4 positions got counted 4! = 24 times. 43,680 ÷ 24 = 1,820.

Name it: "16 choose 4"

Picking 4 spots out of 16 where order does not matter is written C(16, 4). Same move everywhere. Picking 2 reviewers from a team of 6:

Shaun guesses

6 · 5 = 30 ordered picks. The real answer is 15. Why not 30?

"Ana then Ben" and "Ben then Ana" are the same pair of reviewers. Every pair got counted twice. 30 ÷ 2 = 15.

If time: where "3 choose 2" hides

(a + b)(a + b)(a + b) multiplies out to 8 pieces. Three of them — aab, aba, baa — collapse into the same term a²b. That 3 is "3 choose 2": which 2 of the 3 brackets donated an a.

Trap to name

Counting "Ana then Ben" and "Ben then Ana" as two picks. Ordered counting is fine — as long as you remember to divide by how many orders describe the same thing.
