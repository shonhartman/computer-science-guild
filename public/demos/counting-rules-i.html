The one big idea

Count the easy thing.

Some things are too many to count one by one.

So find something easy to count that has the same number. Then count the easy thing.

Every tool today is a different way to play this one game.

A box of 12 doughnuts. The shop has 5 kinds.

Draw each doughnut as a circle. Put a wall between kinds.
Now write each circle as 0 and each wall as 1

Every box makes one row. Every row makes one box. There are 1,820 rows, so there are 1,820 boxes. Nobody had to list them.

Tool 3 · Too many pigeons

More pigeons than holes means someone shares.

10 holes. 11 pigeons. Ten pigeons can each get their own hole. The eleventh has to share.

We know some hole has two. We don't know which one. We never looked. We just counted.

Official name: the pigeonhole principle

For engineers: more keys than hash buckets means a collision. You know it before you look at a single key.

Boston hair

About 500,000 people in Boston have hair. Nobody has more than about 200,000 hairs.

Make one bucket for every hair count. Put 2 people in every bucket.

200,000 buckets × 2 = 400,000 people 100,000 people left over

The leftovers have to go somewhere. So some bucket gets a third person. Three people in Boston have exactly the same number of hairs.

Tool 3 · Again

Two handfuls always tie.

Pick any 10 two-digit numbers. Grab a handful and add them up.

Count the handfuls. Each number is in or out. That's 2 choices, ten times: 1,024 handfuls.

Count the totals. They go from 0 up to 10 × 99 = 990. That's 991 totals.

More handfuls than totals. Two handfuls have to land on the same total.

Official name: a non-constructive proof. We proved it's there without pointing at it.

Tool 4 · Count double, then divide

Count it twice. Then cut it in half.

Two rooks that look exactly the same. They can't share a row or a column.

Rook A can go anywhere. Rook B has to stay out of A's lines.

"A here, B there" and "B here, A there" make the same picture. We counted every picture twice, so we divide by 2.

Official name: the division rule

Watch out: it only works if every picture gets counted the same number of times.

Tool 5 · Choices in a row

Choices in a row multiply.

2 shirts and 3 pairs of pants make 6 outfits. Every shirt goes with every pair of pants.

Choices can shrink as you go. That's fine. To fill 3 jobs from 10 people, you get 10 choices, then 9, then 8.

What matters is that each step always has the same number of choices, no matter who got picked before.

Official name: the product rule

Tool 5 · Again

Almost every dollar bill is broken.

Every bill has an 8-digit serial number. Call a bill broken if any digit shows up twice. Sounds rare. It isn't.

All serial numbers:
10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 100,000,000

No repeats:
10 × 9 × 8 × 7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 1,814,400

That's under 2 bills in every 100. The other 98 are broken.

For engineers: this is why the birthday problem surprises people, and why random IDs collide sooner than you'd guess.

Tool 6 · Separate piles

Separate piles add.

12 kids walk to school. 8 kids ride the bus. Nobody does both. That's 12 + 8 = 20 kids.

If some kids do both, adding counts them twice. Fixing that is next time's lesson.

Official name: the sum rule

Walk: 12
Bus: 8
